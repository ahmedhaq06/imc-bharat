From madrasa to medicine: How one PIN Code Pioneer changed the trajectory of 47 families in Patna

When Dr. Arshad Hussain joined IMC as a Pioneer, his PIN code had zero scholarship applications on record. Eight months later, 47 students had accessed government scholarships, coaching support, and specialist mentorship.

The Challenge

In Bihar's capital Patna, access to quality education for Muslim students from lower-income households has long been constrained by a combination of limited awareness of government schemes, lack of trusted intermediaries, and fragmented community networks. PIN code 800001 — home to over 40,000 residents — had not produced a single verified scholarship application in the three years preceding IMC's arrival.

Families knew opportunities existed in theory. But navigating the bureaucracy of Minority Affairs portals, understanding eligibility criteria, and submitting the right documents required a level of institutional literacy that simply wasn't present at the grassroots level.

📌 Context

Bihar has one of the highest concentrations of Muslim students eligible for central scholarship schemes — yet application rates consistently fall below the national average. The gap is rarely about eligibility. It is almost always about access to guidance.

How IMC Got Involved

In January 2024, Dr. Arshad Hussain — a paediatrician with 14 years of practice in Patna — joined IMC as a PIN Code Pioneer after being introduced to the platform by a colleague in the Founding Council. His motivation was straightforward: he had seen too many bright students from his neighbourhood drop out of education pathways not because of ability, but because of isolation.

Within two weeks of onboarding, Dr. Hussain had mapped 112 households in his locality and identified 63 students between the ages of 14 and 22 who were either enrolled in secondary education or had recently completed their boards.

"I didn't need a grant or a committee. I needed a system. IMC gave me the verified network, the tools, and — critically — the credibility to walk into families' homes and say: I can actually help you."

— Dr. Arshad Hussain, PIN Code Pioneer, Patna (800001)

What Was Done

Over a period of eight months, Dr. Hussain — working with two other IMC members in his locality — ran a structured intervention across three phases:

Phase 1: Mapping and Trust-Building (Weeks 1–4)

Door-to-door visits to 112 households. Students identified, documents inventoried, eligibility assessed against five central scholarship schemes. Families briefed in Urdu and Hindi on what was available and what was required.

Phase 2: Application Support (Weeks 5–16)

Group application sessions held at a local mosque on Friday evenings. IMC's legal council volunteer assisted with Aadhar and income certificate queries. 47 complete, verified applications submitted across NSP, Maulana Azad Fellowship, and state minority schemes.

Phase 3: Follow-Up and Placement (Weeks 17–32)

Regular tracking of application status. Three students — all first-generation college aspirants — were connected to IMC's employment and education network and successfully secured medical college admissions with full scholarship coverage.

Outcomes

47 Scholarship Applications

All verified and submitted through official government portals — a 100% success rate from zero in the same PIN code the year prior.

₹12 Lakhs Unlocked

Combined scholarship value disbursed or committed across 47 students, covering tuition, accommodation, and exam fees.

3 Medical Seats Secured

First-generation college students admitted to accredited medical institutions with full funding — a direct outcome of the IMC mentorship chain.

Replication in 2 PINs

Dr. Hussain's model has since been adopted by two neighbouring PIN Code Pioneers, extending the impact to adjacent localities.

What Made It Work

The single most important variable in this case study was not funding, not technology, and not policy — it was a trusted, verified local individual with institutional backing. Dr. Hussain's existing credibility as a respected doctor in the community meant families were willing to share documents and engage with a process they would otherwise have avoided.

IMC provided the scaffold: the verified member network to connect him with legal and financial experts, the platform to track applications, and the broader council to escalate cases that required specialist intervention. The Pioneer model — one trusted individual per PIN code — is now being validated as IMC's most replicable unit of impact.

"Counted people count. This case study is proof that when a community is visible in data and systems, it becomes impossible to underserve."

— Adnan Safee, Founder, Nine Angle Foundation / IMC

Lessons for the Platform

  • Trust precedes technology. No app or portal replaces a known, respected local face.
  • Friday evening group sessions outperformed individual appointments 3:1 in conversion.
  • Document readiness is the primary bottleneck — not awareness, not willingness.
  • Replication is automatic when the model is simple, documented, and resource-light.
